Scope of the project

A Cyber Range Platform for Strengthening Energy Sector Cybersecurity

A federated cyber range tailored to the energy sector is essential to fortify cybersecurity defenses and safeguard critical energy infrastructure against escalating cyber and hybrid threats.

The project Cyber Polygon: A Cyber Range Platform Specialized on Energy Sector with Automatic Generation of Virtual Scenarios focuses on developing a state-of-the-art cyber range platform designed for seamless integration with organizations' operational technology (OT) environments. By providing a simulated environment, it enables realistic training, rigorous technology testing, and process evaluation under high-pressure scenarios.

Integrating OT systems into the cyber range enhances organizations' preparedness and response capabilities, fostering robust cybersecurity measures.

"By simulating realistic OT environments, Cyber Polygon empowers organizations to test, train, and respond effectively to evolving cyber threats."

Current Challenges

  • Increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks targeting critical infrastructure
  • IT–OT convergence creating new attack surfaces
  • Shortage of skilled OT security personnel
  • Inability to safely test in production environments

The Cyber Polygon Solution

  • Isolated environment for testing and training
  • Realistic, energy-sector-specific scenarios
  • Continuous training for security teams
  • Proactive validation of security measures

From Awareness to Crisis Leadership

01 Awareness & strategy

Introduction to Cybersecurity in the Energy Sector and Cyber Range Capabilities

  • 4 February 2026
  • The Ritz-Carlton, Baku
  • 65 participants

This event covered strategic, organizational and technical sides of cybersecurity in the energy sector. Participants looked at the particular weaknesses of energy infrastructure and at how the Cyber Polygon cyber range can be used to train staff, test security and evaluate incident response without affecting operational systems.

  • Energy-infrastructure risks: legacy systems, patching constraints, supply-chain dependencies and cyber-physical interconnection
  • Resilience through cooperation, information sharing and coordinated response
  • Presentation of the Cyber Polygon platform and its capabilities

Audience: 30 critical infrastructure companies · 2 cybersecurity authorities · 8 research, academic & public-sector bodies

02 Hands-on technical

Cyber Resilience for Critical Energy Infrastructure — Hands-On Cyber Range Exercise

  • 25 June 2026
  • NATO Laboratory, Baku Oil Higher School
  • 29 participants

A practical session for energy-sector operators and technical staff. It opened with an overview of current threats, including ransomware, supply-chain attacks, APTs, AI-assisted attacks and OT/ICS vulnerabilities. Participants then worked through a live exercise on the Cyber Polygon platform in a safe, controlled environment.

  • Threat detection, monitoring and identification of malicious activity
  • Incident triage, containment, mitigation and operational continuity
  • Team coordination during a simulated cyber crisis, followed by a debrief on lessons learned

03 Strategic decision-making

Tabletop Exercise: Cyber Incident Response and Decision-Making for Energy Infrastructures

  • 6 August 2026
  • Baku, Azerbaijan
  • 45 participants

Senior representatives from energy operators, public authorities and cybersecurity bodies worked in four groups: national authorities and regulators, national cyber incident response, energy operators, and international coordination. The scenario started as an unclear anomaly at a gas compressor station. It grew into a national crisis with cross-border energy disruptions, compromised renewable-energy systems, disinformation and the risk of blackouts. Each group's decisions changed how the scenario developed next.

  • Define roles, escalation procedures and communication channels before a crisis occurs
  • Early information sharing builds trust and makes international support easier to get
  • Balance cybersecurity measures against operational continuity
  • Communicate clearly under uncertainty and separate confirmed facts from early assessments

Audience: 17 critical infrastructure companies · 8 cybersecurity authorities · 5 public-sector, research & crisis-management bodies
